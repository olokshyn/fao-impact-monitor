# Kenya: El Niño Risk Outlook

## Past Impacts

Kenya’s agricultural sector is highly exposed to El Niño-related rainfall variability because of the sector’s economic importance and continued dependence on rainfall. Agriculture, forestry and fishing accounted for about 23.2% of GDP in 2025, while agriculture employed about 45.8% of the workforce. Irrigation remains limited, and therefore leaving the sector vulnerable to erratic rainfall: land equipped for irrigation represented only 3.77% of cropland in 2024 [1]. Past El Nino event in 2023-24 showed geographical differences with dual effects: highly above average rainfall in eastern Kenya while below average rainfall observed in western Kenya. This created dual effects: excess rainfall and flooding in some regions and additional pressure on pre-existing drought vulnerability in others.

**Agriculture and food production**: The 2023/24 El Niño event showed how El Nino impacts fed into pre-existing drought vulnerabilities. Cumulative droughts from five consecutive years of below average rainfalls devastated crops, livestock, water availability. Long‑rains main season crops were estimated to be 5 – 10 percent below the five-year average nationally, but with certain areas that faced dry spells experiencing more substantially below-average production [2].

**Food security, nutrition and household wellbeing**: Past events showed that households already experienced high costs for staples from preceding climate shocks as El Niño events developed. The 2023-24 El Nino episodes arrived at a difficult time as cumulative droughts drove wholesale maize prices in major Kenyan markets 30–60% above the five-year average [3]. Retail maize prices in pastoral and marginal agricultural areas were 14–75% above the five-year average. While price pressure cannot be attributed to El Niño alone, they reflected the compounding risks of El Nino and preceding droughts.

## Expected impacts

**Projected losses to agricultural production**: A high likelihood of below-average rainfall across most cropping areas in southwestern Kenya indicates potential losses to cereal crops that are at different stages of development [4].

**Farmers and rural livelihoods are likely to bear the brunt**: El Nino occurs against a backdrop of already high food prices following the energy and agricultural input crunch. 2024 already recorded the highest prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity in total population, almost 74% [5]. Farmers, forest-dependent communities, and fisherfolks had limited access to credit, with less than 4% of total credit going to agriculture, forestry, and fishing in 2024 [6]. This means that a bumper harvest from El Nino compounded by preceding climate shocks can be a make a break for many rural communities with limited access to finance for recovery.

**Impacts of El Nino can generate secondary risks for animal heath and livestock production**: Past El Nino events in 1997-98 [7] and 2023-24 [8] with widespread flooding such as in the southern Kenya introduced conditions conducive to disease outbreaks among life stock. It shows that agricultural impacts from El Niño erratic rainfall can continue through animal-health pathways even after the immediate flooding subsides.

## Preparedness Considerations

The inter-agency drought AA framework has been completed. Activities include the distribution of animal feed and provision of veterinary services to prevent disease outbreaks and reduce livestock mortality during drought. Actions also include the distribution of seeds of short-cycle crop varieties and collapsible water storage tanks, aiming to sustain household food production and improve water access in dry-season grazing areas.

Anticipatory actions for floods in Kenya are designed to protect vulnerable communities, livelihoods and productive assets before rising water levels cause widespread losses and disruption. The approach combines early warning dissemination and community preparedness measures, ensuring that at-risk populations receive timely, impact-based flood forecasts and practical guidance on flood preparedness, public health and disease prevention. To safeguard agricultural production, farmers receive support through the provision of flood-tolerant and short-cycle crop varieties, improved seed and grain storage solutions, farm-level drainage measures, and the pre-positioning of agricultural inputs and tools that enable rapid recovery after floods. Collectively, these anticipatory actions seek to reduce displacement, protect livelihoods and productive assets, limit flood-related disruptions to food production, and strengthen the resilience and preparedness of flood-prone communities ahead of anticipated flood events.

Beyond short-term anticipatory actions, El Niño also presents an opportunity to strengthen resilience through investments in sustainable water management. Priority measures include rehabilitating and expanding water infrastructure, promoting climate-smart irrigation systems, and strengthening Water Users Associations and local water governance to improve long-term management of scarce water resources in arid and semi-arid areas.
